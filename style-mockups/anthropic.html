The problem

Robots have no Common Crawl.

The last decade of AI ran on one fact: give a model enough real human data and it gets good. Language models read most of the internet; vision models saw billions of photographs. Robots have nothing close.

What exists comes from motion-capture studios, lab sets, teleoperation, and simulation — expensive, hard to scale, and missing the part that matters most: how people share space, tools, and tasks in the unpredictable chaos of real work. That is the part that makes a robot easy to be around, and it is exactly what cannot be staged.

How it works

Wear a light kit. Record real work. Own a share.

1

Wear the kit

An MMT core — wide camera, depth, audio, and motion — with a small swarm of body-worn sensors for full-body pose, every source synchronized to the millisecond.

2

Capture verified sessions

You go about ordinary work and control what each session records. Every hour clears automated quality, fraud, and consent checks before it enters the dataset.

3

Own a share

Each verified hour earns a proportional share of what the dataset earns from licensing, for as long as it keeps earning. The earliest contributors hold the largest shares.

For contributors

Turn ordinary daily work into a lasting stake in the data robots are built on. No special skills. The data belongs to the people who generate it.
